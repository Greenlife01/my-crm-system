India spends between ₹1.7 and 2 lakh crore every year subsidising fertiliser imports, and not a single tonne of potash is produced domestically. Every kilogram used on Indian farms is shipped in from abroad, exposing the country's food security to global supply shocks and price volatility.

## The basalt opportunity

Beneath large parts of central India lies one of the most significant volcanic formations on Earth: the Deccan Traps. Basalt rock from this formation is rich in calcium and magnesium silicates, and — critically for farmers — contains meaningful quantities of potassium.

When crushed and spread on farmland, basalt weathers naturally, releasing potassium and other minerals into the soil over time. This isn't a replacement for all fertiliser needs, but it is a powerful, domestically sourced supplement that reduces reliance on imports while improving soil structure.

## A carbon-negative fertiliser input

The same chemical reaction that releases potassium into the soil also draws down atmospheric CO₂, converting it into stable bicarbonate ions. This is the basis of Enhanced Rock Weathering (ERW) — the process Terrasols uses at scale through Project Nirmatva.

For India, this means a single intervention that:

- Reduces potash import dependency
- Improves soil health and crop yield
- Removes carbon permanently from the atmosphere
- Costs farmers nothing

It's rare that a climate solution and a food security solution are the same rock.
